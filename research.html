Research

Measuring brain chemistry with rigor

My research develops and applies in vivo magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) to detect and quantify low-concentration metabolites in the living human brain.

Research program

Methods that translate biosignals into biomedical evidence

Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) complements MRI by noninvasively measuring chemical concentrations in tissue. My work focuses on improving the acquisition, processing, and interpretation of these measurements, with particular attention to GABA and glutathione.

The work connects technical development with biomedical application: better sequences, more reliable processing, consistent reporting, and collaborative studies designed to establish how measurements behave across people, scanners, and sites.

Core question

How can MRS measurements be made more reliable, comparable, and useful?

That question links the methodological, collaborative, and open-science strands of my research.

Research themes

Connected methodology

01

Advanced edited magnetic resonance spectroscopy

Developing and evaluating spectral-editing approaches for low-concentration, overlapping metabolite signals, including multi-metabolite methods such as HERMES and HERCULES.

02

Reliable measurements

Testing repeatability and reproducibility across sequences, scanners, processing choices, and research sites so that biological interpretation rests on robust measurements.

03

Multi-site collaboration

Leading and contributing to coordinated studies such as Big GABA, which brought together 26 institutions to examine implementation differences in edited MRS.

04

Open tools and standards

Developing software and contributing to community standards, including Gannet and BIDS, to support transparent, reusable workflows.
